EARTH

About Earth

Earth is the only planet known to support life. It has air to breathe, water to drink, and the perfect distance from the Sun to keep temperatures comfortable.

Key Facts

  • Third planet from the Sun
  • Has liquid water
  • Supports life

Fun Facts

  • Earth is always moving but we don't feel it
  • Over 70% of Earth is covered by water
  • Earth has one Moon

Planet Connection

Earth is very different from Venus and Mars because it has liquid water and breathable air, which allows life to exist.

Did You Know?

Earth's atmosphere protects us from harmful rays from the Sun.

About This Project

Quick Start Guide

Step 1: Approach the solar system model, scan the QR code located on the base. Step 2: Read the short introduction page explaining how the project works. Step 3: Scan the qr code to open a plannet page. Step 4: The dedicated page opens explains about the planet using calm, educational language and a short quick quiz located at the bottom of the page. Step 5: Navigate through the information on the page and visualise the physical plannets on the project to get an understanding of the solar system. This simple interaction flow ensures that users of all ages can understand and enjoy the functionality of the project both physical as well as digital.

This project is an interactive physical-digital solar system that promotes participation and inquiry-based learning. In order to provide visitors a more engaging and unforgettable understanding of the planets, it combines a handcrafted physical display with digital content.

By enabling individuals to physically visit a planet and then digitally evaluate its data, the objective is to transcend beyond passive learning. Because it encourages curiosity, investigation, and active involvement, this method is particularly helpful for children and school-age children.

Why Earth Was Developed First

Earth has been designed as a fully realized model of how each planet would operate inside the system at this point in the project. Because Earth is well-known to users and offers a solid point of reference for comprehending the distinctions across planets, it was selected initially.

The Earth page demonstrates:

  • The visual style that will be used across all planets
  • The structure of the information (About, Facts, Connections, Quiz)
  • How interaction will guide learning

This page acts as a working prototype for the rest of the solar system.

Digital Earth & 3D Programming

Instead of being a static image, the Earth on this page was produced using 3D programming. This strengthened the sense of movement and realism by making the Earth look like a revolving sphere in space.

Creating the Earth in 3D was a technically demanding process, requiring:

  • Understanding of 3D space and perspective
  • Texturing the surface to represent land and water accurately
  • Positioning lighting to simulate sunlight
  • Optimising performance for smooth interaction

Although this procedure greatly lengthened the project's development time, it was crucial in making sure it felt engaging and immersive rather than flat or merely informative.

Navigation & Interaction Design

There is a "Back to Solar System" button at the top of the page. This button effectively conveys the desired navigation structure of the finished product, even if it is not yet operational. When this button is finished, users will be able to switch between the view of the entire solar system and particular planets with ease.

In order to reinforce learning and motivate users to consider what they have learned, there is a quiz section at the bottom of the page. As a result, the site becomes an active learning tool rather than just a reading experience.

NFC & QR Code Limitation

The initial goal of the project was to use NFC tags inserted into each real globe so that users could touch their device to access digital content.

This interaction could not be carried out at this time due to the delayed arrival of the NFC components. QR codes are being used as a workaround to link the digital content to the physical model.

This restriction is flexible during the development process and has no impact on the project's central idea. The technique is designed to allow for the eventual addition of NFC without altering the digital content's structure.

Project Development & Progress

Each planet in this project's iterative development builds on the one before it. The Earth page shows the system as it is now and gives a good picture of how the finished project will operate.

Future development will include:

  • Completing the remaining planet pages
  • Activating navigation between planets
  • Integrating NFC interaction
  • Refining physical controls and interactivity
  • Making the physical project move and function
  • Creating a digital solarsystem as a front page
Reflection

This project shows how digital design, interface design, and physical fabrication can collaborate to produce educational experiences that are meaningful. The difficulties encountered thus far, such as 3D development and hardware constraints, have led to a better comprehension of both technical and design procedures.
